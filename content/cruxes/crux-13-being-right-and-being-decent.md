title: "Being right and being decent"

**What you're really disagreeing about:** whether treating someone respectfully is a separate obligation from getting the facts right, or whether it's part of the same job.

**How each side would put it:**

> "The truth doesn't change based on how it's delivered. Requiring every correct statement to be gentle is how uncomfortable facts get suppressed, and softening a message often costs the person the information they needed."

> "How you say it determines whether it's heard, so delivery isn't decoration — it's whether the truth arrives at all. And someone can be entirely correct and still treat you as an idiot, which is a real harm independent of the facts."

**Where it shows up:** medical appointments, feedback at work, arguments with teenagers, public debate, and — reliably — inside every other crux in this library, where "you're wrong" and "you're not listening to me" arrive in the same sentence.

**The test:**

1. **Split the sentence.** Ask directly: is the complaint that the conclusion is wrong, or that the exchange was bad? Both can be true. They need different responses and get conflated constantly.
2. **Would being gentler have changed the facts?** If no, delivery is a separate conversation — worth having, but not this one.
3. **Would being gentler have changed whether they heard it?** If yes, delivery is part of the job and the person who was right still failed at something.
4. **Ask yourself:** in this argument, do I want to be understood or do I want to be right? Answer privately and truthfully. It changes what you say next more than any other question here.

**A worked example.** Two people re-litigate an argument they had a month ago. Question 1 reveals one of them has been defending a fact and the other has been describing an insult. Neither has responded to the other in four weeks.

**Where it ends:** if you both accept that correctness and decency are separate obligations, you disagree about what's owed when they conflict. That's a question about what conversation is *for* — and it may be the most useful thing your group ever names out loud, because it governs every other argument you have.
