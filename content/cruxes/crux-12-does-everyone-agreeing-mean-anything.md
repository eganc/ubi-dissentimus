id: crux-12
title: "Does everyone agreeing mean anything?"
type: evidence
surfaces_as:
  - climate
  - nutrition
  - economics
  - medicine
---

**What you're really disagreeing about:** whether broad agreement among qualified people is evidence that something is true, or evidence about how that group forms opinions.

**How each side would put it:**

> "When people who've studied something independently converge on an answer, that convergence is information. It's not proof, but a lone dissenter isn't automatically the brave truth-teller — usually they're just wrong, which is why we notice the rare exceptions."

> "Consensus can form because everyone read the same three papers, trained under the same people, and needs the same funding. Agreement measured inside a group that shares those things measures the group as much as the world."

**Where it shows up:** climate, nutrition, economics, medicine, any argument containing "the science is settled" or "science isn't done by consensus."

**The test:**

1. **Did they arrive independently?** Different methods, different countries, different funding, different decades — that's convergence. Everyone citing the same original study is one data point wearing a crowd costume. Check which one this is.
2. **What happens to a dissenter in this field?** Are there published disagreements, or is the field visibly quiet? Some fields argue in public constantly. That's a health indicator either way.
3. **Has this consensus been overturned before, and how did it go?** Look at the actual history of the field. Some have reversed repeatedly, some haven't reversed in a century. That's checkable and it's rarely checked.
4. **What's the specific claim?** Consensus is usually strong on a narrow core and much weaker on the policy attached to it. People argue as though agreeing with one commits you to the other. Separate them.

**A worked example.** An argument about a scientific claim. Question 4 does the work: one person is defending the core finding, the other is attacking the policy built on top of it. Both are often right. The consensus fight was a proxy.

**Where it ends:** if you both accept that consensus is real evidence and also socially produced, you disagree about the weight — which is a judgment about how much this particular field's incentives distort it. That's not resolvable tonight, but it's a much narrower disagreement than the one you started with.
