**What you're really disagreeing about:** how much present cost is worth bearing for a future benefit — and how confident we have to be about the future to justify it.

**How each side would put it:**

> "Some problems compound. Dealing with them later costs vastly more than dealing with them now, and the people who pay later mostly don't get a vote today. Deferring is a way of passing the bill to someone who can't object."

> "Predictions about the far future are unreliable, and present costs are certain. People suffer now, concretely, so that a projected benefit might arrive for someone else. Wealth and capability compound too — a richer future may solve this more cheaply than we can."

**Where it shows up:** climate spending, government debt, pensions, infrastructure, saving vs. spending, whether to push a kid hard now for a payoff later.

**The test — both people answer:**

1. **How far out do you actually plan?** In your own life — mortgage, retirement, your kids. People are often far more patient privately than the position they argue publicly, and noticing the gap is instructive.
2. **How much does a benefit thirty years out count against one today?** Half? A tenth? Nothing? You've never said. Say it now. Two people with the same facts and different answers here will never converge, and it takes one sentence to find out.
3. **What's your confidence in the projection?** Not whether the direction is right — how much the specific number matters to your answer. If your position doesn't change when the projection is halved, the projection isn't what's driving you.
4. **Who's paying, and who's benefiting?** If they're the same people, this is a savings question. If they're different people, it's a fairness question, and those don't resolve the same way.

**A worked example.** Two people argue about debt. Both answer question 2 with a rough number. One is at "a dollar in thirty years is worth about ten cents today," the other at "about eighty cents." Every downstream disagreement follows from that gap, and it took thirty seconds to find. Neither number is wrong.

**Where it ends:** if your discount rates genuinely differ, you disagree about how much the future counts. That's as close to bedrock as this gets. Say it plainly and go get dessert.
