title: "When does my choice become your business?"
type: value
surfaces_as:
  - mandates
  - helmet and seatbelt laws
  - smoking
  - drug policy
  - gun ownership
  - what your kid is allowed to do at someone else's house

**What you're really disagreeing about:** at what point does an individual decision affect enough other people that the group gets a say.

**How each side would put it:**

> "Some risks aren't personal. If what you do lands other people in hospital, or costs everyone money, it stopped being purely your call. Living together means accepting some limits."

> "Almost anything can be described as affecting others if you draw the circle wide enough. That reasoning has no natural stopping point, and it always ends with someone else deciding what you may do with your own life."

**Where it shows up:** mandates, helmet and seatbelt laws, smoking, drug policy, gun ownership, what your kid is allowed to do at someone else's house.

**The test — both people answer:**

1. **Name the limit you accept.** Everyone accepts some. Seatbelts, drunk driving, building codes. Which ones, and why those?
2. **Name the limit you reject.** Same exercise the other way. Someone who accepts every limit or rejects every limit is arguing a position they don't actually live.
3. **What's the difference between the two you just named?** This is the real question. Almost everyone has a line. Very few have ever said out loud where it is, and hearing your own answer is usually the most useful thing in the session.
4. **Does it change if the numbers change?** If the harm to others were ten times larger, would your answer move? If it wouldn't, you're arguing a principle. If it would, you're arguing a threshold — and thresholds are negotiable in a way principles aren't.

**A worked example.** Two people are stuck on a mandate. Both do questions 1 and 2. One accepts drunk-driving laws but rejects vaccine mandates; the other accepts both. Question 3 surfaces the difference — usually something like directness of harm, or whether the risk is chosen. That's the seam. It's specific, it's honest, and neither person had to call the other reckless or authoritarian to get there.

**Where it ends:** if you've both named your line and they're in different places for reasons neither can dislodge, you disagree about how much weight to give one person's autonomy against a diffuse cost to everyone. Political philosophy has not settled this in three hundred years. You will not settle it before the food gets cold.
